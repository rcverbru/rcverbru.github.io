Bat Detection and Tracking Research

This project develops low-disturbance imaging and detection methods for monitoring hibernating bat populations in the abandoned mines of the Houghton, Michigan area. Using near-infrared and long-wave infrared (LWIR) cameras, the goal is to locate, count, and track bats without disturbing them during hibernation. We aim to build an automated pipeline for turning that imagery into reliable population data so that current methods of hand counting can be retired for current DNR Researchers. This work is being done through the Robust Autonomous Systems Lab (RASL) at Michigan Tech.

This project is in association with Carl Greene, Breanna R. Gusick, and the Michigan DNR.
